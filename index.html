Legally Required Finished Product Testing (Table 7) — cfu/g
How to read n / c / m / M:
n = number of sample units taken (always 5 here)  ·  c = max units allowed to fall between m and M before batch fails  ·  m = standard/target — aim to be below this  ·  M = absolute ceiling — ANY unit above M = automatic fail regardless of others.

Example — Meat Preparations E. coli (the 5000 rule): Take 5 units. Up to 2 may fall between 500–5000 cfu/g (the amber zone). If 3+ fall in that zone OR any single unit exceeds 5000 → batch fails outright. All 5 under 500 = compliant. The "limit" people quote as 5000 is M — the ceiling. The real target (m) is 500.

Appendix 5 — Aseptic Product Sampling Method (6 Steps)
Core principle: nothing must touch the inside of the sample bag at any point except the product being sampled. The bag is used as a sterile barrier.
StepActionCritical Rule

2Open the bag.Nothing must touch the inside of the bag.

4Turn the bag inside out.Nothing must touch the inside of the bag. Your hand is now on the outside of the inside surface — this is the sterile grip.

Appendix 1 — 5 Whys Method
The 5 Whys approach asks "why did the process fail?" rather than simply "why?" — this steers toward actionable process-level root causes rather than classic dead-ends like "not enough time" or "not enough manpower".

Why 3The rubber band has broken.

Scope: The secondary scalding unit — subject of an R&D project to reduce Campylobacter. This is an R&D procedure liable to change — always use the latest issue.

Operating Steps

M7 — Unit Failure
If water will not reach temperature: unit must be drained so processed birds are not passing through water below the required temperature. If the carousel fails: must be repaired before continuation of processing — no alternative.
M9 — Temperature Drop Below 75°C (Critical Response)
If secondary scalder drops below 75°C:
1. Engineering and Business Unit Manager must be informed immediately by the QC in the department.
2. Temperature must be recorded every 5 minutes by the QC Supervisor until temperature returns to 75°C and is stable for 5 minutes.
3. Shift Manager/Factory Manager & General Manager must be made aware.
4. MUST ensure no Retail Whole Birds are packed from when the secondary scalder temperature reduced until it was restored.
5. Technical Director, Managing Director, and Operations Director for Poultry MUST be informed when temperature drops below 75°C (by General Manager or deputy).
M10 — Engineering Requirements
The Steam valve MUST be risk assessed and included on the engineering PPM schedule. MUST also be included on the site Internal Audits.

Salmonella Enteritidis (SE) Salmonella enterica serovar Enteritidis Gram-negative rod bacterium. Group D Salmonella. The most common cause of egg/poultry-linked salmonellosis in the UK. Lives in the gut of chickens (often without the bird showing symptoms). Contaminates carcasses at slaughter. Causes severe gastroenteritis, fever, diarrhoea in humans. Can be fatal in vulnerable people. Absence in 25g — any detection = immediate batch hold, DTC + TD escalation, FSA report, customer notification. n=5, c=0. SOB-25c / TP4177
Salmonella Typhimurium (ST) Salmonella enterica serovar Typhimurium Gram-negative rod. Group B Salmonella. Second key regulated serovar alongside SE in EU/UK poultry regulation. Same route and illness as SE. ST is also a common cause of human food poisoning outbreaks. Legally required to be absent alongside SE in poultrymeat. Absence in 25g — same action as SE above. Fully typed if detected. DTC + TD + FSA. SOB-25c / TP4177 (ID column confirms SE vs ST)
Salmonella spp. (other serovars) All other Salmonella species and serovars Large family — 2,500+ serovars. Most are pathogenic to humans. The specific serovar matters for severity and legal action required. Any Salmonella in mince, meat preparations, and meat products = automatic fail. Not all trigger the same FSA reporting as SE/ST but all require DTC + TD escalation and disposal as Cat 2. Absence — detected = dispose as Cat 2, escalate DTC + TD, FSA report. SOB-25c / TP4177 · ID column for serovar
Campylobacter spp. Principally C. jejuni and C. coli Gram-negative, spiral-shaped, microaerophilic bacterium. The UK's #1 cause of bacterial food poisoning — over 500,000 cases per year. Lives naturally in the intestines of poultry without causing disease in the bird. Spreads to carcasses during slaughter (scalding, plucking, evisceration). Causes human campylobacteriosis: severe diarrhoea, cramps, fever lasting 1–2 weeks. Can lead to reactive arthritis and Guillain-Barré syndrome. Must be enumerated (not just presence/absence). Neck skin: Fail >1000 cfu/g (Table 8). Product: <600 cfu/g Co-op target; >1000 all COP limits exceeded. Caeca: high counts = high-risk flock. Campy1Fc / TP4138 · CampyFnc / TP4138* (presumptive)
E. coli O157 (STEC/VTEC) Escherichia coli O157:H7 (Shiga toxin-producing) A specific dangerous variant of E. coli that produces Shiga toxins. Not routinely in poultry but tested by ASDA and Iceland for red meat products. Can cause haemolytic uraemic syndrome (HUS) — kidney failure, especially in children. Infectious dose is very low (as few as 10 organisms). Primarily a red meat concern but appears in ASDA/Iceland specs. Not Detected (ASDA Cat 2 stuffing and Iceland raw meat specs) — detected = immediate fail. EcPF / TP4167 (confirmed E. coli in some suites)
Listeria monocytogenes Listeria monocytogenes Gram-positive rod. Can grow at refrigeration temperatures (0–4°C) — making it particularly dangerous in chilled, ready-to-eat environments. NOT routinely tested at 2SFG Scunthorpe because all products are raw and require cooking. Cooking destroys Listeria. Not tested (low care raw poultry) — only relevant if site has high care / RTE production. Not in standard suite at Scunthorpe
Bacillus cereus Bacillus cereus Gram-positive, spore-forming rod. Produces heat-stable toxins. Spores survive cooking. Relevant in cereal-based products, ingredients, and stuffing. If detected above limit — must be differentiated from B. thuringiensis (a natural insecticide, not pathogenic). B. cereus causes vomiting and/or diarrhoea. Speciate if above unacceptable limit. Mainly a concern in water samples from surface-influenced sources. Iceland also tests for it in long-life vacuum-packed products (target <100 cfu/g, weekly). These organisms don't generally cause illness themselves, but their presence tells you whether the process is clean. High counts = something is wrong with hygiene, temperature control, or cross-contamination. They drive the swab KPI and corrective action cycle.

E. coli (generic) Escherichia coli (beta-glucuronidase positive, confirmed) Gram-negative rod from the faecal coliform group. The confirmed, specific marker of faecal contamination. Distinct from E. coli O157 above. Presence on product or swabs = faecal contamination at some point in the process. In raw poultry it's expected at low levels, but high counts indicate poor hygiene at slaughter or in cutting. On hand swabs it indicates inadequate hand washing. Useful as an early warning: if Enterobacteriaceae is rising, E. coli and potentially pathogens may follow. Environmental swabs: Fail >100 cfu/swab (Table 4). KFC product: target <10,000, USL 10,000. Enterococci Enterococcus spp. Gram-positive cocci. Inhabit the intestinal tracts of humans and animals. Formerly called Faecal Streptococci. Used in water testing as a definitive marker of faecal contamination. More resistant to environmental conditions than E. coli — can persist in water systems longer. Presence = definitive faecal pollution of water supply. Water: 0 cfu/100ml — High Level Positive if detected. Immediate action. EntWc / TP4137 (water)
Staphylococcus aureus (Coagulase +ve) Staphylococcus aureus (coagulase-positive, confirmed) Gram-positive coccus. Produces heat-stable toxins. Found naturally on human skin, nasal passages, and hands. The key organism for hand swabs. High counts indicate operatives are not washing hands properly or are handling product while carrying a skin/nasal infection. The toxin it produces survives cooking — which is why controlling it at the handling stage matters even for raw products that will be cooked. Hand/PPE swabs: Pass <10, Caution 10–100, Fail >100 cfu/swab (Table 6). ASDA: target <20, report >1000. Iceland Added Value: target <100, OOS 100. SaFc / TP4106
TIER 3 — Spoilage Organisms (Quality · Affect shelf life, not food safety)
These organisms don't generally make people ill from poultry products — but they cause the product to spoil (off-smell, slime, colour change) before the use-by date. High counts = product will fail shelf life. They are the main organisms driving customer complaints about quality.

Pseudomonas spp. Pseudomonas species (principally P. fluorescens and P. fragi) Gram-negative aerobic rods. Highly psychrotrophic — grow very well at refrigeration temperatures (0–4°C). TVC / ACC / Aerobic Colony Count Total Viable Count at 30°C (Total Aerobic Colony Count) A count of ALL aerobic bacteria that can grow at 30°C. Not a specific organism — it's a total load indicator covering hundreds of species simultaneously. The single most important overall hygiene/spoilage indicator. A high TVC means the product has a lot of bacteria of many types — it will spoil faster, and may have elevated hygiene-indicator organisms too. Used both on product (shelf life) and on swabs (process hygiene). Swabs: Pass ≤1000, Fail >1000 (Table 4). Minced Meat legal: m=5×10⁵, M=5×10⁶ (OOS if >40%). KFC: target <5×10⁵, USL 1×10⁶. Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB) Lactobacillus, Leuconostoc, Carnobacterium and related genera Gram-positive, generally anaerobic or microaerophilic bacteria. Produce lactic acid as a metabolic byproduct. Dominant spoilage organisms in vacuum-packed and modified atmosphere products. They create sour smell, slime, and gas in packs. Not a food safety risk but ruins product quality. Rise dramatically as use-by date approaches in MAP packs. Per product specification. No internal 2SFG procedure limit — tracked for shelf life trending. LacFc / TP4114
Yeasts Various yeast species (principally Candida spp., Rhodotorula spp.) Unicellular fungi (eukaryotes). Not bacteria — but grouped with moulds in micro testing as fungal spoilage organisms. Spoilage in coated, marinated, and ingredient products. Also used for air quality testing (spore testing in pre-chill and ingredient packing areas). Co-op has a specific yeast limit on marinated/coated products. Found in stuffing and brine ingredients. Air plates: Acceptable <10, Warning 10–20, Adverse >20 cfu/plate. Co-op product (marinated): target <10,000/g, unacceptable >1,000,000/g. Raw Material Intake (dry): tested per batch. YF / TP4109
Moulds Various mould species (Aspergillus, Penicillium, Fusarium and others) Multicellular filamentous fungi. Grow as visible colonies — the "furry" growth seen on spoiled food. Some produce mycotoxins. Mainly an ingredient and air quality concern. Mould in pre-chill or ingredient packing areas = product contamination risk. High mould counts in dry ingredients (breadcrumbs, stuffing, spices) indicates poor storage or moisture ingress. Air plates: same limits as Yeasts above (<10 acceptable, >20 adverse). Dry intake tested per batch vs specification. MF / TP4109 (same code as Yeasts in some suites)
Bacillus spp. (non-cereus) Bacillus subtilis, B. licheniformis, B. pumilus, B. megaterium etc. Gram-positive, spore-forming aerobic rods. Very heat-resistant spores. Widespread in soil and the environment. Found in ingredients (especially stuffing, spices, breadcrumbs) and occasionally finished products. High counts in stuffing = ingredient quality issue. Not pathogenic (unless confirmed as B. cereus), but cause spoilage and product quality failures. Must be speciated if above limit. If above unacceptable limit: speciate to confirm species. If B. cereus detected — further differentiation from B. thuringiensis required before action. Reported in identification columns (ID / Cspp)
Sulphite Reducing Clostridia (SRC) Anaerobic, spore-forming, Gram-positive rods. Reduce sulphite compounds as part of their metabolism. Found in soil and intestinal tracts. Used only in water testing — specifically for water that originates from or is influenced by surface water. Their presence indicates the water source has been contaminated with soil or faecal material at some point. Customer-specific action if detected.
