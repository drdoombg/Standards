Legally Required Finished Product Testing (Table 7) — cfu/g
How to read n / c / m / M:
n = number of sample units taken (always 5 here)  ·  c = max units allowed to fall between m and M before batch fails  ·  m = standard/target — aim to be below this  ·  M = absolute ceiling — ANY unit above M = automatic fail regardless of others.

Example — Meat Preparations E. coli (the 5000 rule): Take 5 units. Up to 2 may fall between 500–5000 cfu/g (the amber zone). If 3+ fall in that zone OR any single unit exceeds 5000 → batch fails outright. All 5 under 500 = compliant. The "limit" people quote as 5000 is M — the ceiling. The real target (m) is 500.

Appendix 2 — Air Sampling Method
Two methods: Passive (Exposure) — plate left open for 1 hour. Active (Air Sampler Unit) — device samples for ~1 minute. Plates must stabilise at room temperature for 1 hour before exposure. Always include a control plate — must NOT be opened.
StepInstructionKey Detail
1Order plates from labCorrect agar for organism under test. Always include a control plate with each submission.
2Store plates2–5°C refrigerated. Do NOT push to back — may freeze and become unusable. Use within shelf life.
3Stabilise at room temperatureAllow plates to reach room temperature — 1 hour stabilisation before exposing.
4Label the plateNumber on lid. Record number on submission form with location, date, time.
5aPassive methodRemove lid (do not contaminate inside surface or lid). Expose agar for 1 hour (or specified period). Temperature, airflow and humidity affect performance — record conditions. Replace lid. Tape up.
5bActive method (air sampler)Remove device lid → unscrew top section → insert plate with agar facing up → remove plate lid (do not touch media) → screw top back on → press "Yes" until device operates → samples ~1 min → device powers down → remove top → remove plate → replace lid. Tape up. Device will not start if top is not screwed correctly or needs charging.
6Control plateDo NOT open. Label, tape up, return with all sample plates.
7TransportDirect to lab within 20 min (max 1 hr without refrigeration). Or refrigerate: preferably <5°C, max 8°C. Transport within 24 hrs. Results invalid if received >24 hrs or no date/time provided.
Appendix 4 — Environmental Microbiological Swabs
Sampling locations, areas, timing, and technique must follow risk-based principles. Reference standard: ISO 18593:2018. Hygiene swabs must be physically separated from other sample categories to prevent cross-contamination.
StepInstructionKey Detail

2Store swabsUnopened: 4–10°C. Use within shelf life stated on packaging.

Appendix 4 — Hand Swab Method
StepInstruction
1Follow all preparatory steps from the Environmental Microbiological Swabs method above.
2Select operatives randomly from production area on a weekly basis — swab after hand washing station.
3Label the casing with the name or clock number of the person being swabbed, plus other traceability information.

Appendix 5 — Aseptic Product Sampling Method (6 Steps)
Core principle: nothing must touch the inside of the sample bag at any point except the product being sampled. The bag is used as a sterile barrier.
StepActionCritical Rule

2Open the bag.Nothing must touch the inside of the bag.
3Hold the bag by the bottom seam.—
4Turn the bag inside out.Nothing must touch the inside of the bag. Your hand is now on the outside of the inside surface — this is the sterile grip.

Appendix 8 — Listeria Risk Assessment (Primary Sites Summary)
PointDetail
Risk to humansListeria monocytogenes causes listeriosis — serious infection primarily affecting pregnant women, newborns, and immunocompromised adults. Symptoms: fever, muscle aches, GI symptoms; can spread to nervous system causing headache, stiff neck, confusion, loss of balance, convulsions. In pregnancy: miscarriage, stillbirth, premature delivery, neonatal infection.
SourcesSoil and water. Vegetables from contaminated soil or manure. Animals can carry without symptoms and contaminate meats and dairy. Found in raw meats, vegetables, and processed foods contaminated post-processing (e.g. soft cheeses, deli meats).
Key fact for raw poultryListeria is killed by pasteurisation and cooking. Raw poultry is sold raw and must be cooked before consumption — Listeria is therefore considered low risk in low care (primary processing) environments.
Policy for low care sitesListeria is NOT routinely tested in low care facilities. Use-by date and cooking instructions must be clearly printed on all product sold directly to consumers.

Salmonella Enteritidis (SE) Salmonella enterica serovar Enteritidis Gram-negative rod bacterium. Group D Salmonella. The most common cause of egg/poultry-linked salmonellosis in the UK. Campylobacter spp. Principally C. jejuni and C. coli Gram-negative, spiral-shaped, microaerophilic bacterium. The UK's #1 cause of bacterial food poisoning — over 500,000 cases per year. TIER 3 — Spoilage Organisms (Quality · Affect shelf life, not food safety)
These organisms don't generally make people ill from poultry products — but they cause the product to spoil (off-smell, slime, colour change) before the use-by date. High counts = product will fail shelf life. They are the main organisms driving customer complaints about quality.

TVC / ACC / Aerobic Colony Count Total Viable Count at 30°C (Total Aerobic Colony Count) A count of ALL aerobic bacteria that can grow at 30°C. Not a specific organism — it's a total load indicator covering hundreds of species simultaneously. The single most important overall hygiene/spoilage indicator. A high TVC means the product has a lot of bacteria of many types — it will spoil faster, and may have elevated hygiene-indicator organisms too. Used both on product (shelf life) and on swabs (process hygiene). Swabs: Pass ≤1000, Fail >1000 (Table 4). Minced Meat legal: m=5×10⁵, M=5×10⁶ (OOS if >40%). KFC: target <5×10⁵, USL 1×10⁶. Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB) Lactobacillus, Leuconostoc, Carnobacterium and related genera Gram-positive, generally anaerobic or microaerophilic bacteria. Produce lactic acid as a metabolic byproduct. Dominant spoilage organisms in vacuum-packed and modified atmosphere products. They create sour smell, slime, and gas in packs. Not a food safety risk but ruins product quality. Rise dramatically as use-by date approaches in MAP packs. Per product specification. No internal 2SFG procedure limit — tracked for shelf life trending. LacFc / TP4114
Yeasts Various yeast species (principally Candida spp., Rhodotorula spp.) Unicellular fungi (eukaryotes). Not bacteria — but grouped with moulds in micro testing as fungal spoilage organisms. Spoilage in coated, marinated, and ingredient products. Also used for air quality testing (spore testing in pre-chill and ingredient packing areas). Co-op has a specific yeast limit on marinated/coated products. Found in stuffing and brine ingredients. Air plates: Acceptable <10, Warning 10–20, Adverse >20 cfu/plate. Co-op product (marinated): target <10,000/g, unacceptable >1,000,000/g. Raw Material Intake (dry): tested per batch. YF / TP4109
Moulds Various mould species (Aspergillus, Penicillium, Fusarium and others) Multicellular filamentous fungi. Grow as visible colonies — the "furry" growth seen on spoiled food. Some produce mycotoxins. Mainly an ingredient and air quality concern. Mould in pre-chill or ingredient packing areas = product contamination risk. High mould counts in dry ingredients (breadcrumbs, stuffing, spices) indicates poor storage or moisture ingress. Air plates: same limits as Yeasts above (<10 acceptable, >20 adverse). Dry intake tested per batch vs specification. MF / TP4109 (same code as Yeasts in some suites)
Bacillus spp. (non-cereus) Bacillus subtilis, B. licheniformis, B. pumilus, B. megaterium etc. Gram-positive, spore-forming aerobic rods. Very heat-resistant spores. Widespread in soil and the environment. Found in ingredients (especially stuffing, spices, breadcrumbs) and occasionally finished products. High counts in stuffing = ingredient quality issue. Not pathogenic (unless confirmed as B. cereus), but cause spoilage and product quality failures. Must be speciated if above limit. If above unacceptable limit: speciate to confirm species. If B. cereus detected — further differentiation from B. thuringiensis required before action. Reported in identification columns (ID / Cspp)
Sulphite Reducing Clostridia (SRC) Anaerobic, spore-forming, Gram-positive rods. Reduce sulphite compounds as part of their metabolism. Found in soil and intestinal tracts. Used only in water testing — specifically for water that originates from or is influenced by surface water. Their presence indicates the water source has been contaminated with soil or faecal material at some point. Customer-specific action if detected. Water: 0 cfu/100ml — customer specific action if detected. SRClW / TP4143 (water only)
